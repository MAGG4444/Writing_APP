# Character Archetypes

Distilled from the user's writing craft library after reading the source text. This skill is original guidance for the writing agent; do not quote or reproduce the books.

## Use This Skill For

- Building `characters.md`.
- Designing a protagonist-antagonist contrast.
- Giving side characters a useful dramatic function.
- Checking whether a character has become only a label or trope.

## Core Thesis

An archetype is not a stock template. It is a pressure pattern: what a character values, fears, protects, hides, and becomes under stress.

Do not write "a typical X." Write a person whose dominant drive expresses itself through class, culture, age, history, body, work, relationships, and private shame.

## Character Build Frame

For every major character, define:

- **Dominant pattern**: the archetypal energy that takes over under pressure.
- **Primary value**: what they would sacrifice comfort, status, safety, or love to protect.
- **Fear**: the loss or humiliation they secretly organize life around avoiding.
- **Drive**: the human need behind the behavior: belonging, respect, knowledge, self-expression, safety, control, freedom, justice, etc.
- **Public mask**: how other people describe them.
- **Private logic**: how they justify their choices to themselves.
- **Shadow form**: how the same strength becomes manipulation, cruelty, avoidance, control, vanity, martyrdom, passivity, or self-destruction.
- **Growth test**: the situation that forces them to act against their default pattern.

## Archetype Use Rules

- Choose the archetype that creates the most growth against the plot obstacle.
- Do not let the archetype dictate the plot subject. Let it shape dialogue, subtext, reaction, priorities, and blind spots.
- Combine archetype with personal past. Fear is stronger when it has a remembered source.
- A character may contain several patterns, but one should dominate in crisis.
- Design contrast: two characters can want similar outcomes but pursue them through incompatible drives.
- A good antagonist is often the protagonist's power without restraint, or the protagonist's fear turned outward.

## Supporting Role Functions

Use side characters to pressure the protagonist rather than fill space:

- **Mentor**: gives tools or tests, but may withhold information to force growth.
- **Friend**: offers loyalty while tempting the protagonist to remain unchanged.
- **Lover/intimate bond**: exposes vulnerability and demands a personal choice.
- **Rival**: competes for the same prize or identity.
- **Shadow**: says or does what the protagonist represses.
- **Lost soul**: shows what the protagonist may become if they refuse change.
- **Ideal mirror**: shows the person the protagonist wants to become, envy, or surpass.
- **Trickster/catalyst**: disrupts the protagonist's control and reveals hidden desire.

## Journey Patterns

Use these as transformation shapes, not biological rules:

- **Power-gaining arc**: a character begins trapped in obedience, denial, dependence, false safety, or borrowed goals, then claims agency.
- **Control-releasing arc**: a character begins by proving, conquering, protecting, earning, or controlling, then must surrender a false measure of worth.

The agent should pick the arc that fits the character's starting wound and ending insight.

## Scene-Level Character Tests

Before writing a scene involving a major character, ask:

- What does this character think they are protecting?
- What are they actually protecting?
- What would embarrass or expose them here?
- What does their body, timing, silence, or avoidance reveal?
- What would their shadow version do if they felt cornered?
- What small action shows the pattern without naming it?

## Output Checklist

When using this skill, include:

- Character function in the story.
- Dominant pattern, value, fear, drive, mask, and shadow.
- Past pressure that shaped the pattern.
- Current goal and hidden need.
- Relationship pressure with at least two other characters.
- Growth test and likely failure mode.
- One concrete scene behavior that reveals character without exposition.
