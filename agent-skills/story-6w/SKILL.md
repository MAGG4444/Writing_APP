# Story 6W

Distilled from the user's writing craft library after reading the source text. This skill is original guidance for the writing agent; do not quote or reproduce the books.

## Use This Skill For

- Developing an idea into `outline.md`, `characters.md`, `world.md`, and `goals.md`.
- Checking whether a chapter plan has a real cause-and-effect chain.
- Preventing an outline from becoming only events without emotional stakes.

## Core Thesis

A story is not a sequence of interesting events. A story is what those events mean to one protagonist, how they pressure an existing inner contradiction, and how that pressure changes later choices.

Always track two rails:

- **External rail**: visible situation, goal, obstacle, consequence.
- **Internal rail**: belief, fear, misreading, wound, value conflict, new realization.

If an event does not affect the internal rail, it may be plot noise.

## The 6W Frame

Use the six questions in order.

1. **What If**: Identify the specific pattern-break. It must disturb a stable plan, not merely add a strange event.
2. **Who**: Choose the protagonist whose life and false certainty are most damaged by that disruption.
3. **Why**: Explain why the disruption matters to this person because of their past, not only because it is objectively dangerous.
4. **Worldview**: State the protagonist's operating belief before the story begins. This belief should once have protected them but now limits them.
5. **What Next**: Make each event cause the next through choice, interpretation, and consequence.
6. **When**: Define the point where delay becomes impossible: deadline, threat, invitation, exposure, loss, or moral cost.

## Project Material Method

When developing an idea into project materials, produce:

- **Premise**: one sentence combining disruption, protagonist, stakes, and irony.
- **Before-story wound**: what happened before chapter one that created the protagonist's current worldview.
- **Visible desire**: what the protagonist thinks they want.
- **Hidden need**: what they must understand or admit.
- **False belief**: the private rule they use to stay safe.
- **External problem**: the plot-level conflict that forces action.
- **Internal problem**: the belief or fear the external problem keeps pressing.
- **Opening pressure**: why the story starts now.
- **Ending insight**: the realization the story is moving toward.

## Scene Card

For each chapter or major scene, fill these fields before drafting:

- **Alpha point**: the scene's immediate purpose.
- **What happens**: the visible event that starts the scene.
- **Why it can happen**: logic that makes the event possible in this world.
- **Why it happens to this character**: link to desire, fear, past, or mistake.
- **External consequence**: what changes in the situation by the end.
- **Internal consequence**: what the viewpoint character understands, misunderstands, fears, or decides.
- **Next trigger**: how the consequence creates the next scene.

## Causality Tests

For every plot beat, ask:

- Why does this need to happen?
- Why can it happen under the current rules?
- Why would this character cause, allow, notice, hide, or resist it?
- What does it force the character to do next?
- What previous wound, belief, or relationship makes the reaction specific?

Reject beats that only exist because the outline needs them.

## Planning Rules

- Build the first chapter from the moment an old coping strategy stops working.
- Let backstory appear as cause, pressure, memory, lie, or missing context, not as biography.
- Know the ending insight early; it guides what the opening must plant.
- A scene may be quiet, but it must still shift the reader's understanding or the character's options.
- Subplots and side characters should apply pressure to the protagonist's internal rail, not simply decorate the world.

## Output Checklist

When using this skill, include:

- Premise.
- Protagonist, visible goal, hidden need, false belief.
- Past event or pattern that makes the present pressure personal.
- Opening disturbance and cost of refusal.
- Ending insight or likely transformation.
- 3-5 causal next beats, each with external and internal consequences.
